Fewer than 1 in 3 American students read at grade level (Nation's Report Card, 2025). The decline started before COVID. The early years are where it can be fixed.

Read the full case →

In January 2025 the Nation's Report Card confirmed what educators had been watching for a decade: fewer than one third of American students read at grade level. Scores have declined by a full grade level in some states since 2013 — and COVID accelerated a collapse that was already underway. The research is unambiguous: the trajectory is set early, the gap opens before kindergarten, and the families with the least information pay the most for it. NearBase exists to close the information half of that gap — starting with the military families who restart their search from zero every two to three years.

"The difference between a great teacher and a bad teacher for a single year is an entire year's worth of learning. Great teachers produce 1.5 years of learning. Bad ones, 0.5." — Waiting for Superman, 2010. Still true in 2026.

Charters held stable through the decline. The strongest early programs — Montessori, bilingual, play-based — are exactly what most families can't find.
